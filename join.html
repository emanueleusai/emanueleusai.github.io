Join the lab

Build a detector. Search for new physics.

My group at The University of Alabama works on the CMS experiment at CERN’s Large Hadron Collider. We test hardware for the CMS detector upgrade, analyze LHC data, and build AI tools for particle physics. I’m recruiting graduate and undergraduate students.

For students

What you would do

Undergraduates

Test detector boards for CMS

In our HGCAL lab you would check hexaboards, the hexagonal readout boards for a new part of the CMS detector, inspecting them under a microscope camera and running electrical tests on their readout chips.

Graduate students

Analyze LHC data

Each graduate student in the group works on an analysis of CMS collision data, such as four-top production or a search for new heavy quarks, with tools that include machine learning and high-performance computing.

Why this group

Why join the group

  • Your work goes into CMS. The hexaboards that undergraduates test in our lab go into the detector CMS will use at the High-Luminosity LHC.
  • Students present their own work, with talks and posters at meetings of the American Physical Society (APS) in 2025 and 2026 and of its Southeastern Section (SESAPS) in 2024 and 2025.
  • Undergraduate research can lead to graduate school here. Nathan Nguyen, an undergraduate researcher in our lab from 2024 to 2026, received the 2024 US CMS HL‑LHC Detector Upgrade Essential Contribution Award and the 2026 Randall Outstanding Undergraduate Research Award. He has been a graduate student in the group since May 2026.
  • An advisor active in CMS. I am a convener in the Top Quark Physics group, following about 20 analyses.

Research projects

Projects and skills

My group works at both ends of a particle physics result: testing detector parts at the lab bench, and turning collision data into results published in journals.

Detector hardware

Help build the CMS High Granularity Calorimeter

For the High-Luminosity LHC, CMS is replacing the calorimeters at both ends of its detector (the parts that measure particle energies) with the High Granularity Calorimeter (HGCAL). My lab, which I set up in 2024, is in charge of quality control for about 10,000 of its hexagonal readout boards, or hexaboards.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Electronics testing
  • Visual inspection
  • Quality control
  • Silicon detectors
How we test a board

Data analysis

Measurements and searches at the LHC

Our current analyses of CMS collision data:

  • Four-top production: four top quarks made in one collision. The top quark is the heaviest known elementary particle.
  • Searches for new heavy quarks: pairs of T′ quarks, hypothetical heavy partners of the top quark.
  • Quantum entanglement between particles made in the same collision, such as two Z bosons.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Python
  • C++
  • ROOT
  • Statistics
  • Working in a large collaboration
More on our research

Machine learning

AI for particle physics

We use machine learning to improve how particles are reconstructed and how collision events are identified in CMS data. This builds on my earlier work on end-to-end deep learning with a team at Google Research, in which neural networks learn to identify particles directly from low-level detector data.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Machine learning
  • Neural networks
  • Python
  • Statistics
AI in my research

Computing

High-performance computing

Analyzing LHC data and training neural networks take a lot of computing power. I set up a high-performance computing cluster at UA dedicated to CMS data analysis and machine learning, in service since May 2024. You would learn to run your analysis and training jobs on it and to work with large data sets.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Linux
  • Cluster computing
  • Large data sets
  • C++
