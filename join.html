Research projects

What you could work on

In particle physics, a result begins with detector parts on a lab bench and ends as a measurement in a journal, and my group works at every step. Here is what that could mean for you.

Detector hardware

Build the CMS High Granularity Calorimeter

For the High-Luminosity upgrade of the LHC, CMS is replacing the calorimeters at both ends of its detector (the parts that measure particle energies) with the new High Granularity Calorimeter (HGCAL). My lab is in charge of quality control for about 10,000 of HGCAL’s hexagonal readout boards, or hexaboards.

You would inspect boards under a microscope camera on a motorized stage, run electrical tests on their readout chips, and help automate the process. Undergraduates in the group start here.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Electronics testing
  • Silicon detectors
  • Lab automation
  • Python

Data analysis

Analyze LHC data

Each PhD student in the group works on an analysis of CMS collision data, as part of a collaboration of more than 3,000 physicists. Our current analyses:

  • Four-top production: collisions that make four top quarks at once. The top quark is the heaviest known elementary particle.
  • Searches for new heavy quarks: pairs of T′ quarks, hypothetical heavy partners of the top quark.
  • Quantum entanglement: measured between particles produced in the same collision, such as two Z bosons.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Python
  • C++
  • ROOT
  • Statistics
  • Large collaborations

Machine learning

AI for particle physics

We use machine learning to improve how particles are reconstructed and how collision events are identified in CMS data.

This builds on my earlier work: end-to-end deep learning, developed with a team at Google Research, in which neural networks learn to identify particles directly from low-level detector data; and machine learning that reduces the human effort needed to certify the quality of CMS data.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Machine learning
  • Neural networks
  • Python
  • Statistics

Computing

High-performance computing

Analyzing LHC data and training neural networks take a lot of computing power. In 2024 I set up the University of Alabama’s high-performance computing cluster for CMS data analysis and machine learning.

You would learn to run your analysis and training jobs on the cluster and to work with large data sets.

Skills you’ll learn

  • Linux
  • Cluster computing
  • Large data sets
  • C++
