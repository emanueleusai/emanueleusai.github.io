Teaching

Teaching physics, a hundred students at a time

At The University of Alabama I teach the calculus-based introductory physics sequence, usually to classes of about 100 students, as well as modern physics, classical mechanics and the graduate physics seminar. I also mentor the students who do research in my group, and I have taught machine learning and data analysis well beyond campus.

Courses

Courses at The University of Alabama

The course I teach most often is PH105, Introduction to Physics with Calculus I, the first semester of the calculus-based introductory sequence. I have taught it five times since Spring 2023, each time to about 100 students, and in Fall 2026 I am teaching it alongside the second semester, PH106.

I have also taught Introduction to Modern Physics (PH253) and Intermediate Classical Mechanics (PH302), and since Fall 2025 the Graduate Physics Seminar (PH597). Altogether that is 695 student enrollments in my undergraduate courses since Spring 2023.

How I teach

Training and feedback

In June 2023 I attended the Faculty Teaching Institute of the American Association of Physics Teachers (AAPT) in Washington, DC.

Every year since 2023, faculty colleagues have observed my classes through peer teaching observations. For every course I teach, I also complete a Student Learning Outcomes assessment, which measures how well the class met the learning goals set for the course.

Teaching also continues outside the lecture hall, with the students who do research in my group.

Mentoring

Mentoring students in research

I supervise one postdoctoral researcher and, as main advisor, three graduate students, who work on CMS data analysis and on the construction of the CMS High Granularity Calorimeter (HGCAL). Since 2024, five undergraduates have done research in our HGCAL lab, testing and inspecting the hexagonal readout boards (hexaboards) we quality-control for the detector.

Outreach

Teaching beyond the classroom

Outside my courses, I have taught machine learning and data analysis to physicists in training, helped run open-source research programs for students, and brought particle physics to high school students and the public.

2021–2026 · Open-source science

Google Summer of Code

Google Summer of Code pairs new contributors with mentors on open-source projects. I co-administer two of its organizations: Machine Learning for Science (ML4SCI) since 2021 and HumanAI since 2024. In 2021 I coordinated 19 ML4SCI projects applying machine learning to scientific research, including quantum generative models and quantum convolutional neural networks for physics data. That year ML4SCI had 5% of all projects submitted to the program.

2016–2022 · Training CMS physicists

Schools at Fermilab's LHC Physics Center

At the CMS Data Analysis School at the LPC, which trains collaborators who are starting out in CMS, I was a facilitator in 2016, 2020, 2021 and 2022, on machine learning and on a search for a hypothetical heavy Z′ boson decaying to a top quark–antiquark pair. For experienced collaborators, I taught the machine learning course at the LPC Hands-on Advanced Tutorial Sessions (HATS) in June 2020 and 2021.

2018–2021 · Competitions

Machine learning hackathons

I co-organized the 2018 LHC ML Hackathon, a three-day competition in the style of Kaggle, where teams race to build the best model on a shared data set from the Large Hadron Collider, followed by the ML4SCI Hackathons in 2020 and 2021. In 2021 the competition opened to other fields of science and ran for three weeks.

2018–2019 · High school

CS4RI Summits

Computer Science for Rhode Island (CS4RI) brings computer science learning to high school students, with a particular focus on groups underrepresented in STEM. At its summits in December 2018 and December 2019, I prepared and delivered an hour-long lecture on machine learning applied to physics. More than 200 students attended my lectures in total.

2018–2019 · The public

Big Bang Science Fair

An outreach event organized by Brown University's high-energy physics group, with more than 2,000 attendees. In September 2018 and September 2019 I was an organizer and exhibitor, and coordinated the LHC exhibit.

2023–2026 · At UA

Bringing students into research

I have recruited undergraduate researchers at UA's Faculty Research Showcase (2023, 2024), and staffed the physics booth at the BCAS Open Day (2026) and the department's graduate recruitment table at SESAPS (2023, 2025). I also served as a judge at UA's Undergraduate Research and Creative Activity (URCA) Conference in 2023, 2024 and 2025.

Since 2024, five undergraduates have worked in our lab, testing and inspecting hexaboards for the CMS detector upgrade, and students from the lab have presented their work at APS and SESAPS meetings.
